Ledger, Redline, Tape

Three visual directions for the same screen, each rendered with real figures from the same six companies. The point is to pick a world, not a colour scheme — each one comes from something this subject actually is.

What the current mock does wrong: soft-shadowed cards, rounded corners, a stat-tile strip, serif headings over a sans interface, muted blue accent. Competent, and identical to every generated dashboard. None of the three below use a card or a shadow.
01

Ledger

Continuous-form accounting paper — banded rows, sprocket margins, ruled columns
02

Redline

The document marked up as a lawyer marks one — struck-through and underlined, not colour-coded
03

Tape

Trading-desk density — rules instead of boxes, everything tabular

How to choose

Ledger suits reading figures and treats the page as a printed record — strongest for the financial tables, weakest for long prose. Redline is the only one where the diff is shown the way the change actually reads, and it handles long text best — but it is a document, not a console. Tape packs the most on screen and scans fastest across many companies, at the cost of warmth and of comfortable reading. They can also be mixed: Tape for the overview, Redline for a single filing.
