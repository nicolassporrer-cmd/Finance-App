What this does not yet do well

  • S&P 500 membership is proprietary. There is no authoritative free source for the constituent list, so it comes from a community-maintained dataset and is recorded with its provenance in data/universe.json. Every CIK is re-checked against SEC's own ticker map rather than trusted from that source, because a wrong CIK silently pulls another company's financials and looks entirely normal on screen.
  • EBITDA and free cash flow are derived, not reported. Neither is a GAAP concept. EBITDA is operating income plus D&A; FCF is operating cash flow minus capital expenditure. For Microsoft, Alphabet, Broadcom and Oracle, D&A is itself reconstructed by summing depreciation and amortisation of intangibles, because they never tag a combined figure.
  • Fourth quarters are reconstructed. No company files a Q4 10-Q — Q4 exists only inside the annual figure. Bars marked □ are full year minus the three filed quarters, not a filed number.
  • Period labels are derived from the fiscal year end, not from XBRL's own fy field — that field records the fiscal year of the filing that reported the fact, so NVIDIA's 2024, 2025 and 2026 year-ends all carry fy2026 and would have printed the same year on three different years of data.
  • Five companies have almost no history at their current CIK. APA, BNY, FedEx Freight, Honeywell Aerospace and ExxonMobil have reorganised, and SEC has moved the ticker to a newly registered entity — XOM now resolves to "ExxonMobil Holdings Corp", whose predecessor's ticker list is empty. The pipeline follows SEC's authoritative mapping, which is correct and also means those sheets are nearly bare. They are flagged in red at the top of the sheet.
  • Three banks have no single revenue tag. Truist, Synchrony and Regions file net interest income and noninterest income separately with nothing summing them, so revenue is reconstructed from the two. Goldman Sachs needed a different tag entirely (RevenuesNetOfInterestExpense) and had no revenue at all until it was added.
  • MD&A is found in 95% of 10-Qs and 92% of 10-Ks, not all. JPMorgan's 10-Q contains no "Management's Discussion" heading at all, and a long tail of filers lay the section out in ways the title match misses. Those companies show no language comparison rather than a wrong one.
  • Only 20% of 10-Qs restate Risk Factors. The other 80% cross-reference their 10-K instead, so most quarterly language comparisons cover MD&A alone. That is a real difference in filing practice, not a parsing failure — and which companies bother is itself informative.
  • Dual-class listings are collapsed in the ranking only. Alphabet sits in the index as both GOOG and GOOGL with identical figures; the top twenty counts it once, by CIK. Both still appear under their letters.
  • Analyst consensus is ratings only, and shallow. Finnhub's free tier returned recommendation counts for all 503 companies, but price targets sit behind its paid tier and return 403, so no target range is shown anywhere. It also returns only four monthly snapshots, so the mix chart is a short window, not a long trend.
  • The index hides what most companies are doing. The S&P is capitalisation-weighted, so a handful of large winners can hold it near flat while the typical company falls. That is why the top bar reports breadth — how many rose, how many fell, the median move, and how many moved more than 5% — beside the index level. Read them together: an index down 1% with a third of the market down 5% is a different week from a quiet one.
  • Short-window returns count trading days, not calendar days. "1 week" is five sessions back, "1 month" twenty-one. Counting calendar days would land on weekends and holidays and silently compare against the wrong close. Where the history is too short to reach back, the cell is blank.
  • The 30-session chart is separate from the two-year one for a reason. The long series is thinned to fortnightly to keep the page loadable, so a one-week move fits inside a single step of it and cannot be drawn. The recent chart keeps full daily resolution.
  • Price reaction is not attribution. It is the move from the last close before the filing to the first close after it. The earnings release, guidance and market-wide moves land in the same window.
  • Company descriptions are quoted, never written. Every sentence under "What this company does" comes verbatim from that company's own Item 1 "Business" in its most recent 10-K, with the filing linked. A convincing one-line summary of what a company does is trivial to generate and impossible for a reader to tell apart from a sourced one, so none is generated: a company whose Item 1 cannot be parsed shows an explicit blank instead. The selection is automatic, so a few companies open on a less useful sentence than a human would have picked.
  • The dynamics panel is a set of measurements, not a recommendation. Each line compares two filed figures and states which two. It is not investment advice and does not attempt a verdict.
